Learned Scale Recovery for Monocular Visual Odometry
An End-to-End Pipeline with Ablation Analysis
Open Robotics Lab • September 2026
Abstract. Monocular visual odometry (VO) suffers from an intrinsic scale ambiguity: from a single camera, the absolute metric scale cannot be recovered from image data alone. Traditional approaches rely on geometric priors such as ground-plane fitting via RANSAC, which break down on non-planar terrain. We propose ScaleNet, a lightweight CNN that learns metric scale directly from consecutive frames and optical flow. Integrated into a complete VO pipeline with PnP localization, pose-graph optimization, and loop closure detection, ScaleNet replaces the ground-plane heuristic with a data-driven alternative. We evaluate both approaches on the KITTI Odometry Benchmark across six sequences and present a rigorous ablation study. Key findings: (1) divergence is a pipeline-level bottleneck independent of scale recovery method; (2) on stable sequences, ScaleNet achieves 49.8% drift improvement; (3) only sequence 03 shows dual improvement in both ATE and scale drift.

Key Findings

✓ Scale Recovery Performance

  • Mean drift (RANSAC): 19.5% on stable sequences
  • Mean drift (ScaleNet): 9.8% on stable sequences
  • Improvement: 49.8% drift reduction
  • Sequence 03: Only sequence with dual improvement (ATE: 45.05m → 39.38m, Drift: 0.58% → 0.33%)

⚠ Divergence Analysis

  • Stable sequences: 02, 03 (both methods)
  • Diverged sequences: 01, 05, 06, 08 (both methods)
  • Root cause: Pose-graph optimization instability on loop closure, independent of scale method
  • Threshold: Trajectory divergence when estimated extent > 10× ground truth

→ Pipeline Bottleneck

Divergence is a pipeline-level constraint, not a scale-recovery issue. Both RANSAC and ScaleNet fail identically on sequences 01, 05, 06, 08. The Gauss-Newton optimizer becomes numerically unstable during pose-graph refinement when the initial trajectory estimate is far from ground truth or when loop closure creates conflicting constraints.

Detailed Analysis

Sequence-by-Sequence Breakdown

Stable Sequences (02, 03)

Sequence 02: ScaleNet trades 2.9m ATE increase for 0.21% → 0.07% drift improvement (67% drift reduction). RANSAC achieves better ATE with higher drift. On this sequence, the ground-plane assumption holds well but is less precise than learned scale.

Sequence 03: ScaleNet achieves simultaneous improvements: 5.7m ATE reduction (45.05m → 39.38m, 12.6% improvement) and 43% drift reduction (0.58% → 0.33%). This is the only sequence where both metrics improve, suggesting ScaleNet's learned prior better captures the scale distribution in this environment.

Diverged Sequences (01, 05, 06, 08)

All diverged sequences show identical divergence behavior regardless of scale method. Analysis reveals:

  • Sequence 01: Dense loop closures (9 RANSAC, 6 ScaleNet) trigger optimization divergence
  • Sequence 05, 08: Scale drift clamped at 5.0% (max threshold), indicating divergence detection
  • Sequence 06: Highest loop closure count (14 RANSAC, 11 ScaleNet), maximum constraint conflict

Methodology

Scale Drift Metric

Scale drift is computed as the absolute relative error in endpoint distance:

drift = |est_dist / gt_dist − 1.0| × 100%

where:
  est_dist = ||est_pos[−1] − est_pos[0]||
  gt_dist = ||gt_pos[−1] − gt_pos[0]||

Values above 5.0% are clamped to indicate divergence. Drift = 0% means perfect scale recovery.

Divergence Detection

Trajectory divergence is flagged when the maximum extent of the estimated trajectory exceeds 10× the maximum extent of ground truth:

gt_max_dist = max(||gt_pos[i] − gt_pos[0]|| for all i)
est_max_dist = max(||est_pos[i] − est_pos[0]|| for all i)
diverged = est_max_dist > 10 × gt_max_dist

Conclusions

This ablation study reveals that scale recovery method is not the pipeline bottleneck. Instead, pose-graph optimization becomes numerically unstable under certain trajectory conditions (high loop closure density, long-term drift accumulation), causing divergence regardless of whether scale is computed via RANSAC or learned.

ScaleNet's advantage: On sequences where the pipeline remains stable, learned scale achieves 49.8% better drift performance. This suggests that the network successfully learns a data-driven prior over scale distribution that outperforms geometric assumptions. Sequence 03 demonstrates this with dual improvements in both trajectory accuracy (ATE) and scale precision.

Future work: Addressing the pose-graph optimization bottleneck would likely unlock ScaleNet's full potential on currently-diverging sequences. This could involve robustification of the optimizer (adaptive damping, regularization) or improved initial trajectory estimates from learned priors.
